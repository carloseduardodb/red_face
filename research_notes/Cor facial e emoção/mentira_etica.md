# Deception detection from the face (thermal, color/blood flow, micro-expressions, automated video): evidence, limits, and legal/ethical context (EU + Brazil)

Research date: 2026-11-02. Notes compiled for a project considering ordinary (RGB) video of faces. Where a DOI is given without a fetched URL, it comes from well-established bibliographic records; anything I could not verify is listed under Gaps.

## 1. Thermal imaging and deception (Pavlidis 2002, Granada "Pinocchio effect", NRC 2003, field tests)

### Takeaway
Thermal "lie detection" rests on small pilot studies. The US National Research Council found the founding Nature study "does not provide acceptable scientific evidence". The only airport field test found that thermal data added nothing beyond interviewers' judgments. The Granada "Pinocchio effect" work is a single lab paradigm, popular coverage describes it inconsistently, and I found no independent replication.

### Cited Findings
- Pavlidis, Eberhardt & Levine (2002), "Seeing through the face of deception", *Nature* 415:35 (DOI 10.1038/415035a). It reported warming of the periorbital region during deception in a mock-crime polygraph setting at the US DoD Polygraph Institute: 6 of 8 "guilty" participants (75%) and 11 of 12 innocent participants (92%) were classified correctly, about 20 volunteers in total — [UW summary](https://staff.washington.edu/chudler/decept.html); [ScienceDaily 2002](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2002/01/020103083716.htm); [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/health-news/health-technology/the-flushed-face-of-falsehood-505361.html)
- Even contemporary coverage noted that the mechanism was unknown and that the signal might reflect general fight-or-flight arousal rather than deception itself — [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/health-news/health-technology/the-flushed-face-of-falsehood-505361.html); [UW summary](https://staff.washington.edu/chudler/decept.html)
- The NRC (2003) report *The Polygraph and Lie Detection* (ch. 8) gives the history: the DoDPI pilot began with 30 examinees and dropped 5, leaving 12 deceptive and 13 nondeceptive. The Nature paper "uses only a subset of the examinees and offers no information on the selection process" and "gives no information on the decision criteria". It "remains a flawed and incomplete evaluation based on a small sample, with no cross-validation of measurements and no blind evaluation", and "does not provide acceptable scientific evidence to support the use of facial thermography in the detection of deception" — [NRC 2003, ch. 8](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/8)
- In DoDPI's own analysis of the same data, the polygraph scored an accuracy index of A≈0.88 and thermal signals alone A≈0.70, with "some incremental information" when the two were combined — [NRC 2003, ch. 8](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/8)
- On the general question, the NRC concluded: "The meta-analytic literature fails to identify any pattern of facial or body movement that generally signals deception." It did not rule out future research on behavioural indicators — [NRC 2003, ch. 8](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/8)
- Warmelink, Vrij, Mann, Leal, Forrester & Fisher (2011), "Thermal imaging as a lie detection tool at airports", *Law and Human Behavior* 35:40–48 (DOI 10.1007/s10979-010-9251-3). 51 passengers at an international airport lied or told the truth about their trip. Accuracy from thermal scores combined with interviewer judgments was the same as interviewer judgments alone, and the thermal method was less accurate than interviewers — [Lancaster ePrints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/59275/); [Lawfare overview](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/overview-us-lie-detection-systems-airport-security-checkpoints)
- Nervous but honest travellers raise the risk of false positives, and the method has to measure very small changes in skin temperature that the environment can disturb — [Patexia/Veridical summary](https://services.patexia.com/feed/thermal-imaging-lie-detection-and-nervous-flyers-82); [Frontiers Hum Neurosci 2013 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00070/full)
- Granada: Moliné, Dominguez, Salazar-López, Gálvez-García, Fernández-Gómez, De la Fuente & Gómez Milán (2018), "The mental nose and the Pinocchio effect: Thermography, planning, anxiety, and lies", *J. Investigative Psychology and Offender Profiling* (DOI 10.1002/jip.1505). During lying, nose temperature fell by 0.6–1.2 °C and forehead temperature rose by 0.6–1.5 °C, which the authors attribute to mental workload and anxiety — [UGR news](https://canal.ugr.es/noticia/pinocchio-effect-detecting-when-a-person-is-lying/); [LabNews](https://labnews.co.uk/pinocchio_effect_confirmed_lying_affects_the_nose/)
- Earlier Granada press coverage (2012) described the opposite direction, a nose temperature *increase* tied to insula activation when lying about feelings. The reported direction of the effect therefore varies by study and paradigm — [UGR / Science Blogs 2012](https://canal.ugr.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/medios-digitales/science-blogs-eeuu/pinocchio-effect-when-you-lie-your-nose-temperature-rises); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/body-odd/pinocchio-wasnt-lie-nose-reacts-when-you-fib-flna1C7520501)

### Inferences
- The thermal signals reported (periorbital warming, nose cooling, forehead warming) are best read as markers of arousal, cognitive load, or stress. Truthful people under scrutiny produce these states too, so they are not specific to deception. This is the same specificity problem the NRC identified for the polygraph.
- Ordinary RGB video cannot measure skin temperature at all, so this literature does not carry over directly to a webcam project. And even with real thermal cameras the evidence is weak.

### Gaps
- I did not find a formal meta-analysis of thermal deception detection, or independent replications of the Granada nose effect.
- I did not retrieve the full text of Warmelink et al. 2011, so its exact accuracy figures are not reported here (secondary sources mention figures under 70%).

## 2. Facial blood flow / transdermal optical imaging (Kang Lee / NuraLogix)

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed transdermal optical imaging (TOI) papers show that ordinary video can capture facial hemoglobin and blood-flow patterns that vary with induced emotions or physiological state. The claim that TOI detects lies (about 85%) appears mainly in press, TED, and company material. I found no independent peer-reviewed validation of it.

### Cited Findings
- Liu, Luo, Zheng, Wu & Lee (2018), *Scientific Reports*, "Transdermal optical imaging revealed different spatiotemporal patterns of facial cardiovascular activities" (DOI 10.1038/s41598-018-28804-0). This is a proof-of-concept study that extracted hemoglobin-concentration dynamics from standard face video — [PMC6043515](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6043515/)
- A 2022 multispectral face-video study classified emotional states (joy, sadness, disgust, fear, neutral) induced by film clips from "transdermal cardiovascular spatiotemporal facial patterns" — [PMC9249872](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9249872/). A related paper, "Discrete emotions discovered by contactless measurement of facial blood flows", reports similar results — [PhilPapers](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/FUDED)
- Lee presents lying as a "real Pinocchio effect": blood flow falls on the cheeks and rises on the nose, with about 85% accuracy claimed for lie detection. These claims appear in media and company-linked coverage — [U of T News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/professor-ted-speaker-kang-lee-his-new-startup-will-change-how-we-detect-emotion); [TIAP](https://tiap.ca/2016/02/the-real-pinocchio-effect/); [Vice/Motherboard](https://motherboard.vice.com/read/nuralogix-smartphone-lie-detector-toronto)
- Lee himself said the technology would not replace court-grade lie detection, and that the accuracy target ("extremely high, like genetic tests") had not yet been reached — [U of T News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/professor-ted-speaker-kang-lee-his-new-startup-will-change-how-we-detect-emotion)
- The technology is commercialised by NuraLogix and is patented as a "system and method for determining human emotions" — [USPTO patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12230060); [AUTM story](https://www.autm.net/about-tech-transfer/better-world-project/bwp-stories/transdermal-optical-imaging- (toi))

### Inferences
- Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) and TOI-type signals from RGB video plausibly measure physiology: heart rate, perfusion, maybe arousal. Going from there to "deception" adds the same non-specificity problem as thermal imaging and the polygraph. The commercial company that owns the method also has a conflict of interest.
- For this project, facial colour or blood flow is at most a physiological arousal proxy. It is not a lie signal.

### Gaps
- I could not locate a peer-reviewed Kang Lee paper reporting TOI lie-detection accuracy (in children or adults) with methods and sample size. The 85% figure is unverified. Lee's well-known peer-reviewed work on children's lying is behavioural, not TOI-based; this was not verified here.

## 3. Micro-expressions (Ekman; Porter & ten Brinke 2008) and the evidence against

### Takeaway
Micro-expressions are rare, occur in truthful and deceptive people alike, and training to spot them does not improve lie detection. A randomized test of Ekman's METT found trained people were no better than people given bogus training, with accuracy slightly below chance.

### Cited Findings
- Porter & ten Brinke (2008), "Reading between the lies: Identifying concealed and falsified emotions in universal facial expressions", *Psychological Science* 19(5):508–514 (DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02116.x). Partial micro-expressions were rare and appeared in both genuine and falsified expressions (figures from memory of the paper, about 2% of ~697 expressions analysed; see Gaps). Burgoon summarises the related evidence: "few microexpressions in either group" (liars vs truth-tellers) in high-stakes pleas, and "false negatives are commonplace" — [Burgoon 2018, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6158306/)
- Burgoon (2018), "Microexpressions are not the best way to catch a liar", *Frontiers in Psychology* 9:1672 (DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01672). Microexpression theory depends on a chain of unproven propositions: deception produces emotion, emotion produces a leaked expression, and the expression is detectable. Micro-expressions can be "masked, minimized, exaggerated, or neutralized" — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01672/full)
- Jordan, Brimbal, Wallace, Kassin, Hartwig & Street (2019), "A test of the micro-expressions training tool: Does it improve lie detection?", *J. Investigative Psychology and Offender Profiling* 16(3):222–235 (DOI 10.1002/jip.1532). 90 participants were randomised to METT, bogus training, or no training. In Bayesian analyses the METT group did not outperform the other two, and overall accuracy was slightly below chance. METT is part of TSA's SPOT airport screening programme — [Williams College PDF](https://web.williams.edu/Psychology/Faculty/Kassin/files/Jordan%20et%20al%20(2019)%20-%20METT.pdf); [Huddersfield news](https://www.hud.ac.uk/news/2019/september/mett-lie-detection-tool-flaws-street-huddersfield/)
- An earlier Laurentian thesis (Pote 2013) also found that METT training had no effect on detecting masking smiles — [Laurentian](https://laurentian.scholaris.ca/handle/10219/2084)

### Inferences
- Even a perfect automated micro-expression detector would still face the core problem. Micro-expressions are not reliably present in liars and are not absent in truth-tellers, so a highly accurate expression detector does not yield an accurate lie detector.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the Porter & ten Brinke full text in this session. The "~2% of 697" figure is from prior knowledge and should be checked against the paper before quoting.

## 4. Meta-analyses of deception cues and human accuracy

### Takeaway
Across hundreds of studies, people detect lies only slightly better than chance (54%), and most nonverbal cues have weak or null links to deception. The few larger effects are probably inflated by publication bias and small samples (Luke 2019). The face has no "Pinocchio's nose".

### Cited Findings
- Bond & DePaulo (2006), "Accuracy of deception judgments", *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 10(3):214–234 (DOI 10.1207/s15327957pspr1003_2). Covers 206 documents and 24,483 judges. Mean accuracy was 54% (47% of lies and 61% of truths classified correctly). There was a truth bias, and observers did not do better with video than with audio — [PubMed 16859438](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1207/s15327957pspr1003_2); [full PDF via ACLU FOIA](https://assets.aclu.org/live/uploads/document/foia/2006-Personality-and-Social-Psychology-Review-Accuracy-of-Deception-Judgements.pdf)
- DePaulo, Lindsay, Malone, Muhlenbruck, Charlton & Cooper (2003), "Cues to deception", *Psychological Bulletin* 129(1):74–118 (DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.129.1.74). Of 158 cues, most showed no or weak links with lying. The NRC also concluded that no facial or body pattern generally signals deception — [NRC 2003](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/8)
- Hartwig & Bond (2011), "Why do lie-catchers fail? A lens model meta-analysis of human lie judgments", *Psychological Bulletin* 137(4):643–659 (DOI 10.1037/a0023589). Poor accuracy comes mainly from the weakness of the behavioural cues themselves, not from people relying on the wrong cues — [PDF via ACLU FOIA](https://assets.aclu.org/live/uploads/document/foia/2011-07-Psychological-Bulletin-Why-Do-Lie-Catchers-Fail.pdf)
- Luke (2019), "Lessons from Pinocchio: Cues to deception may be highly exaggerated", *Perspectives on Psychological Science* (DOI 10.1177/1745691619838258). A reanalysis plus Monte Carlo simulations shows that publication bias and small numbers of estimates can greatly inflate effect sizes for cues. The paper warns against using cues with "unusually large" effects in practice — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691619838258/)
- Vrij and colleagues have moved the field towards interview-based approaches that increase the liar's cognitive load and focus on verbal content (Warmelink/Vrij's airport work is one example). Human interviewers using a structured protocol beat thermal imaging — [Lancaster ePrints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/59275/)

### Inferences
- Any supervised model trained on face cues inherits a weak and noisy ground-truth signal. Reported accuracy far above about 60–70% from face-only features should be treated as a likely artefact (leakage, confounds, small n) until shown otherwise on independent, held-out subjects.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the DePaulo 2003 and Hartwig & Bond 2011 full texts this session. The details above come from prior knowledge and bibliographic records, and specific effect sizes are not quoted.
- I did not retrieve a specific Vrij review (for example Vrij, Hartwig & Granhag 2019, *Annual Review of Psychology*) this session.

## 5. Automated systems (iBorderCtrl/Silent Talker, AVATAR, EyeDetect) and ML deception datasets

### Takeaway
Deployed or piloted automated "lie detectors" have weak, mostly developer-run validation. iBorderCtrl was based on a 32-person study claiming 75% accuracy, flagged an honest journalist as a liar, and drew lawsuits over its secrecy. The academic ML benchmarks are tiny (121 courtroom clips from about 56–58 people; 35 subjects in Bag-of-Lies) and prone to identity leakage, so high reported accuracies are unreliable.

### Cited Findings
- **iBorderCtrl / Silent Talker (EU Horizon 2020).** An avatar interview analysed facial "micro-gestures", and the Manchester developers' study tested it on 32 people with a reported 75% accuracy (76% per New Scientist, aiming for 85%) — [The Intercept 2019](https://theintercept.com/2019/07/26/europe-border-control-ai-lie-detector/); [Fortune 2018](https://fortune.com/2018/11/02/ai-lie-detectors-eu-countries)
- An Intercept reporter answered every question honestly at the Serbia–Hungary border pilot and was still flagged as lying on 4 of 16 answers. Experts called the system "not credible" or pseudoscience. EU data protection supervisor Giovanni Buttarelli raised concerns about ethnic discrimination. Travellers normally do not see their results, and the reporter got hers only via a GDPR data-access request — [The Intercept 2019](https://theintercept.com/2019/07/26/europe-border-control-ai-lie-detector/); [Intercept Brasil (PT)](https://www.intercept.com.br/?p=261365)
- Sánchez-Monedero & Dencik, "The politics of deceptive borders: 'biomarkers of deceit' and the case of iBorderCtrl", *Information, Communication & Society* 25(3):413–430 (DOI 10.1080/1369118X.2020.1792530). Their Bayesian / base-rate analysis shows that mass screening for rare deceivers is very unlikely to work in practice — [arXiv 1911.09156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09156); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/132993)
- Litigation: in Case T-158/19 *Breyer v REA* (December 2021), the EU General Court recognised a public interest in democratic oversight of surveillance technologies. It still allowed commercial interests to shield information on the technology's reliability and discrimination risk. The Court of Justice (C-135/22 P, 7 September 2023) dismissed Breyer's appeal while setting out transparency principles — [EDRi](https://edri.org/our-work/european-court-supports-transparency-in-risky-eu-border-tech-experiments/); [Patrick Breyer (advocacy)](https://www.patrick-breyer.de/en/posts/iborderctrl/); [Statewatch](https://www.statewatch.org/news/2021/february/eu-secrecy-of-border-control-lie-detector-research-project-examined-in-court); [Article 19](https://article19.org/resources/eu-research-into-biometric-technologies-must-be-transparent)
- **AVATAR (Univ. of Arizona / DHS).** A kiosk interviewer tested in labs, airports and border crossings, with Canada's CBSA trialling it. Reported accuracy ranges from 70% to 92% depending on source, and field reports come from the developers' own centre — [Future Travel Experience](https://www.futuretravelexperience.com/2017/01/canadian-border-services-agency-testing-lie-detection-avatar/); [Eller/UA field test PDF](https://eller.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/FieldTestsofanAVATARInterviewingSystemforTrustedTravelerApplicants.pdf)
- **Converus EyeDetect** (eye and pupil based). The company claims 86–90% accuracy, mostly from developer-affiliated research. Critics call it "the same old polygraph" with the same bias problems. US government agencies (including DoD, CIA and State) have used or evaluated it — [The Intercept 2023](https://theintercept.com/2023/04/07/lie-detector-dod-cia-converus); [Slashdot/WaPo-based report](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/21/11/17/2023212/a-utah-company-says-its-revolutionized-truth-telling-technology-experts-are-highly-skeptical); [Bush School/Texas A&M evaluation announcement](https://soldiersystems.net/2024/02/17/bush-school-team-awarded-project-to-study-effectiveness-of-lie-detection-system-that-tracks-eye-behavior/)
- **Real-life Trial dataset** (Pérez-Rosas et al., ICMI 2015). 121 courtroom clips (61 deceptive, 60 truthful as commonly reported). Deceptive clips mostly come from defendants and truthful clips from witnesses in the same trials, which confounds role with veracity — [ICMI paper](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~zmohamed/PDFs/Trial.ICMI.pdf)
- The Real-life Trial set has only about 58 identities (56 by another count), and each identity is often uniformly truthful or deceptive. A model can therefore "degenerate to person re-identification" if the same person appears in both train and test splits — [Face-Focused Cross-Stream Network, arXiv 1812.04429](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.04429); [Deception Detection in Videos, arXiv 1712.04415](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.04415)
- A Stanford CS224N replication of Gogate et al. (2017), which reported over 90% accuracy, found methodological flaws. The authors argued that identity leakage would justify "skepticism about all results involving the Real-Life Trial Dataset" — [Stanford project](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs224n/cs224n.1244/final-projects/JackRyan.pdf)
- A study comparing low-stakes and high-stakes video datasets notes that the Real-life Trial set is the only public high-stakes video dataset, which raises questions about whether results generalise — [arXiv 2211.13035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13035)
- **Bag-of-Lies** (Gupta et al., CVPR Workshops 2019). 35 subjects and 325 samples (163 truths, 162 lies), with video, audio, EEG and gaze, from a lab task describing images. Later reported accuracies are modest, for example about 60% without EEG and about 66% with all modalities — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2019/html/CV-COPS/Gupta_Bag-Of-Lies_A_Multimodal_Dataset_for_Deception_Detection_CVPRW_2019_paper.html); [arXiv 2307.06625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.06625)

### Inferences
- The recurring weaknesses are tiny n, few unique identities, identity or role confounds, lab "sanctioned lies" with low stakes, no subject-independent test sets, and publication of best-case results. Base rates are a further problem: with rare deceivers among many honest people, even 75–90% accuracy produces mostly false positives in screening.
- Any project evaluation must use subject-independent (leave-subjects-out) splits and report results at realistic base rates.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the original Silent Talker peer-reviewed paper (Rothwell et al., 2006, *Applied Cognitive Psychology*, from prior knowledge) or iBorderCtrl's final evaluation results.
- I found no outcomes from the Texas A&M / Oak Ridge EyeDetect evaluations.

## 6. Why emotion detection ≠ lie detection (Barrett et al. 2019)

### Takeaway
Even inferring *emotion* from facial movement is unreliable. A configuration such as a scowl is neither reliably produced by a given emotion nor specific to it, and its meaning varies with context and culture. Deception is a further inference beyond emotion: liars need not feel any particular emotion, and truth-tellers under suspicion often feel fear, anxiety or stress (the "Othello error"). So face-based deception inference stacks two weak inferences.

### Cited Findings
- Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez & Pollak (2019), "Emotional expressions reconsidered: Challenges to inferring emotion from human facial movements", *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 20(1):1–68 (DOI 10.1177/1529100619832930; corrigendum DOI 10.1177/1529100619889954). The evidence does not support the common view that emotional states can be readily inferred from facial movements. The authors recommend the neutral term "facial configuration" — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.html); [PDF](https://affective-science.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/barrett-et-al-2019-emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.pdf); [Waisman summary](https://childemotion.waisman.wisc.edu/2019/07/22/weaknesses-in-emotion-expression-research-outlined-in-new-report/)
- Facial thermal and blood-flow signals change with arousal and fight-or-flight responses, which are not specific to lying — [HealthDay on Pavlidis](https://www.healthday.com/health-news/health-technology/the-flushed-face-of-falsehood-505361.html); [UGR on anxiety/workload explanation](https://canal.ugr.es/noticia/pinocchio-effect-detecting-when-a-person-is-lying/)
- The NRC's critique of the polygraph is that its physiological measures reflect arousal rather than deception, and the committee applied the same evaluation standard to facial thermography — [NRC 2003](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/8)
- Burgoon: the chain "deception → emotion → leaked expression" breaks at every link — [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01672/full)

### Inferences
- A defensible project framing is "measuring facial physiological or expressive signals (for example rPPG-derived heart rate or perfusion changes, facial action units) under controlled conditions". It should not be framed as inferring hidden mental states, intentions or truthfulness.
- The term "Othello error" (Ekman's own term for misreading a truthful person's fear as guilt) is not cited from a source fetched here; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- I fetched no primary source for the "Othello error" term in this session (it originates in Ekman's *Telling Lies*).

## 7. Legal, ethical and regulatory context: EU AI Act, Brazil (LGPD, PL 2338/2023, ANPD)

### Takeaway
In the EU, AI that infers emotions in workplaces and education is prohibited (Art. 5(1)(f), in force since 2 Feb 2025, except for medical or safety reasons). Emotion recognition elsewhere, and "polygraphs or similar tools" used in law enforcement and migration or border control, are high-risk (Annex III). In Brazil, biometric data is sensitive personal data under the LGPD (art. 5, II; processing under art. 11). PL 2338/2023 passed the Senate in December 2024: it classifies biometric emotion recognition as high-risk, not prohibited. As of late 2026 it was still pending in the Câmara dos Deputados, so it has no legal force yet.

### Cited Findings
- **EU AI Act (Reg. (EU) 2024/1689), Art. 5(1)(f).** Prohibits AI systems that infer the emotions of natural persons in the workplace and in education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons. Commission guidelines read the ban as also covering inference of *intentions*, and as tied to the processing of biometric data. Emotion recognition outside these settings is high-risk rather than banned — [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/); [CMS on Commission guidelines](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-commission-issues-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-part-ii); [Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102kfql/eu-ai-act-unpacked-25-european-commission-releases-critical-ai-act-implementati)
- **EU AI Act Annex III.** Point 1(c): "AI systems intended to be used for emotion recognition" are high-risk. Point 6(b), law enforcement, and point 7(a), migration, asylum and border control: AI systems used by or on behalf of authorities "as polygraphs or similar tools" are high-risk. This covers iBorderCtrl-type systems — [AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Commentators note that the AI Act did not ban AI lie detectors at borders, only classified them as high-risk — [People of Color in Tech](https://peopleofcolorintech.com/articles/ai-lie-detectors-at-borders-who-does-the-eus-ai-act-actually-protect/)
- **Brazil, LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018).** Art. 5, II lists biometric data as "dado pessoal sensível" (sensitive personal data), which carries higher-risk processing rules. ANPD's *Radar Tecnológico* vol. 2, "Biometria e Reconhecimento Facial" (24 June 2024), defines biometrics to include behavioural traits such as facial expression, and flags training-data bias and the risk of biometric template leaks — [Mayer Brown summary](https://www.mayerbrown.com/pt/insights/publications/2024/07/biometrics-and-facial-recognition-anpd-releases-report)
- **PL 2338/2023 (Senate substitute, Sen. Eduardo Gomes).** Classifies as high risk "sistemas de identificação e autenticação biométrica para o reconhecimento de emoções" (biometric identification and authentication systems for emotion recognition), excluding authentication whose sole purpose is confirming a specific person. High-risk obligations include testing documentation, human oversight, bias mitigation, and algorithmic impact assessment — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/pt/insights/publications/2024/07/biometrics-and-facial-recognition-anpd-releases-report); [Senado document](https://legis.senado.leg.br/sdleg-getter/documento/download/69a3ab85-11a1-4713-9d01-80df186d936a); [IA Locus 2026 guide](https://ialocus.com.br/blog/post-pl-2338-marco-legal-ia-brasil-2026.html)
- Conflicting claim: one legal text says emotion recognition at work or in education is in PL 2338's "risco excessivo" (prohibited) list. That contradicts the sources above, which classify it as high-risk, so check the official text before relying on either.

### Inferences
- A Brazilian project processing face video of identifiable people handles sensitive personal data under the LGPD. It needs an art. 11 legal basis (typically specific, highlighted consent, or another listed basis), plus purpose limitation, data minimisation and, in practice, a data protection impact assessment (RIPD).
- If PL 2338 is enacted in its Senate form, any emotion-recognition component would face high-risk obligations. If the project ever served EU users, workplace or education uses would be prohibited and border or law-enforcement "lie detection" would be high-risk.
- Ethically, the scientific weakness above is itself a harm vector. False accusations, discrimination (as the EDPS warned), and opaque results (as in iBorderCtrl) are documented risks. Labelling outputs as "deception" is unjustified by the evidence.
