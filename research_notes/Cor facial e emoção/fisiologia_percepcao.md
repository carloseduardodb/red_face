# Physiology and perception of facial skin color changes linked to emotion and health (excluding Benitez-Quiroz et al. 2018 PNAS)

Research date: 2026-11-02. Labels used below:
- [verified]: confirmed in this session from a primary source, abstract, or publisher or repository record.
- [secondary]: taken from a review or citing paper.
- [bibliographic, unverified]: a reference I know well whose citation details I did not re-check in this session. The report writer should check the DOI before quoting these.

## 1. Blushing, flushing, pallor: physiology, regional distribution, key studies

### Takeaway
Emotional facial reddening comes from active, partly sympathetic vasodilation in facial skin. That skin is unusually rich in superficial vessels. Anger, embarrassment and joy raise facial blood flow. Fear is linked to pallor (vasoconstriction). Blushing appears roughly 15-20 s after an embarrassing event begins. It shows mainly on the cheeks, forehead, ears and neck, and is less visible on darker skin.

### Cited Findings
- Drummond (1999), women under provocation: facial blood flow rose together with cardiovascular and electrodermal activity. The authors concluded that "sympathetically mediated vasodilatation in facial blood vessels competes with cutaneous vasoconstriction during anger". They proposed that facial dilation could act as a "safety valve" against rising blood pressure. [verified, abstract] — [Drummond 1999, Psychophysiology, Murdoch record](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Facial-flushing-during-provocation-in-women/991005543742007891) (DOI 10.1017/S0048577299980344, [bibliographic, unverified])
- Drummond & Quah (2001), Chinese vs Caucasian participants: facial blood flow rose during anger expression whatever the speech style, but fell when neutral material was read aloud. [verified, abstract] — [Drummond & Quah 2001, Psychophysiology, Murdoch record](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-effect-of-expressing-anger-on/991005541297107891)
- Drummond (1994): facial blood flow rose at the start of mental arithmetic, and cheek blood flow rose after a monetary bonus. The authors say this supports the idea that the face "flushes with joy". People high in trait anger reported more anger and embarrassment, but their physiological responses did not differ from low-trait-anger people. [verified, abstract] — [Drummond 1994, Aust J Psychol, Murdoch record](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-effect-of-anger-and-pleasure/991005545431607891)
- Drummond (1997), adrenergic blockade and blushing/flushing: this study tested the sympathetic (beta-adrenergic) contribution to blushing. [verified, record only; I did not retrieve the specific results] — [Drummond 1997, Psychophysiology, Murdoch record](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-effect-of-adrenergic-blockade-on/991005543775307891)
- Drummond (1997, Personality and Individual Differences): self-reports link flushing to anger and embarrassment, and pallor to fear. [secondary] — [Liao, Sakata & Paramei 2022, i-Perception, DOI 10.1177/20416695221080778](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695221080778); [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00525/full)
- Review evidence: facial skin blood flow rises with anger (Drummond 1999; Drummond & Quah 2001), joy (Drummond 1994), anxiety (Hirota et al. 2017; Brummelman et al. 2018; Nikolić et al. 2018) and embarrassment (Drummond et al. 2003, 2007; Drummond & Bailey 2013). It fell during a comedy film. [secondary] — [Frontiers in Psychology 2023, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1259928](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1259928/full)
- Biopsychosocial challenge/threat framing: approach emotions (anger, happiness, surprise) are said to bring vasodilation and a redder, yellower face. Avoidance emotions (fear, sadness, disgust) are said to bring vasoconstriction and a bluer or greener face. [secondary] — [Liao et al. 2022, i-Perception](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695221080778)
- Shearn, Bergman, Hill, Abel & Hinds (1990), "Facial coloration and temperature responses in blushing", Psychophysiology 27:687-693. Citing papers attribute two points to it: a visible blush appears about 15-20 s after an embarrassing episode begins, and facial coloration is linked to temperature changes. [secondary] — [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00525/full) (DOI 10.1111/j.1469-8986.1990.tb03194.x, [bibliographic, unverified])
- Cheek temperature is now used as a blushing index. Watching videos of oneself singing warmed the cheeks more than watching others. fMRI linked this warming to activity in areas tied to emotional arousal and early visual attention, not to mentalizing areas. [verified, abstract] — [Proc R Soc B 2024, "The blushing brain", PubMed 39013420](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39013420/)
- Leary, Britt, Cutlip & Templeton (1992), "Social blushing", Psychological Bulletin 112:446-460. This is the standard review. It defines blushing as triggered by undesired social attention and appeasement-type situations, and describes its spread over the face, ears, neck and upper chest. [bibliographic, unverified] — [DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.112.3.446](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.112.3.446)

### Inferences
- The physiology predicts direction: redder with anger, embarrassment and joy, paler with fear. It also predicts location: cheeks, forehead and ears first. Emotion-color mappings in perception studies (section 3) follow this pattern.
- Facial blood flow can respond in opposite directions within the same emotion, because facial vasodilation competes with general cutaneous vasoconstriction (Drummond 1999). Pallor and flushing are therefore not simple opposites. Visible color will vary across people and situations.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve full texts of Shearn 1990, Drummond 1997 or Leary 1992. The exact regions measured (for example cheek vs ear) and effect magnitudes are unconfirmed here.
- I found no source in this session that quantifies the sympathetic mechanism of blushing, such as the beta-adrenergic or cholinergic contributions. The Drummond 1997 blockade results need the full text.

## 2. Stephen, Perrett, Re, Whitehead and colleagues: redness/yellowness, oxygenated blood, health/attractiveness/aggression/dominance; Changizi et al. 2006

### Takeaway
Observers consistently make faces redder, mainly along the oxygenated-blood color axis, and somewhat yellower, to make them look healthy and attractive. They push redness further still for aggression and dominance. Perceptual thresholds for these changes are tiny, under about 1.5 ΔE. Changizi et al. argue that primate trichromacy is tuned to detect oxygenation and blood-concentration changes in bare skin.

### Cited Findings
- Stephen, Coetzee, Law Smith & Perrett (2009), PLoS ONE 4:e5083. The study used 51 calibrated photographs of Caucasian faces. Participants raised the oxygenated-blood color by ΔE 1.52 ± 0.13 and the deoxygenated-blood color by ΔE 0.51 ± 0.19 to maximize healthy appearance. 98% of faces looked healthier with more oxygenated color, against 66% with more deoxygenated color. When both axes were free, participants lowered the deoxygenated color (ΔE −3.10) and raised the oxygenated color (ΔE +3.03). In a cross-cultural task with 20 Black South African and 18 white UK participants, observers raised a* by 2.39 ± 0.18 across East Asian, Black and white faces. Neither face ethnicity nor participant ethnicity had a main effect. The interaction suggested an own-group effect, with African participants adding more redness to African faces. Blood colors were calibrated with a spectrophotometer, using arm raising and lowering for deoxygenated blood and warm water for oxygenated blood. [verified] — [Stephen et al. 2009, PLoS ONE, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0005083](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0005083)
- The same paper cites literature linking skin blood perfusion and oxygenation to fitness, sex hormones, and to lower blood flow in hypertension, diabetes, ageing and smoking. This study did not test any of these. [verified, background statements] — [Stephen et al. 2009](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0005083)
- Re, Whitehead, Xiao & Perrett (2011), PLoS ONE 6:e17859, "Oxygenated-blood colour change thresholds for perceived facial redness, health, and attractiveness". In a two-alternative forced-choice staircase task with 26 participants and 4 faces, the mean discrimination thresholds were ΔE 0.67 (SEM 0.09) for redness, ΔE 1.44 (0.16) for health and ΔE 1.38 (0.17) for attractiveness. The redness threshold was significantly lower than the other two (d ≈ 0.8). The stimulus continuum spanned ±ΔE 4.8. Photo calibration error was ΔE 1.97, larger than the redness threshold. [verified] — [Re et al. 2011, PLoS ONE, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0017859](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0017859)
- Stephen, Oldham, Perrett & Barton (2012), Evolutionary Psychology 10(3):562-572. Female participants adjusted CIELab a* on men's faces. They added the most redness to maximize perceived aggression, a middle amount for dominance, and the least for attractiveness. Redness raised all three perceptions. [verified, abstract/repository] — [St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/3266) (DOI 10.1177/147470491201000312, [bibliographic, unverified])
- Changizi, Zhang & Shimojo (2006), Biology Letters 2:217-221. The paper argues that primate trichromacy may have evolved to detect skin color changes in conspecifics. Skin color varies along two blood-related dimensions, blood concentration and oxygen saturation. Trichromats can see both, dichromats only one. The M and L cone peak sensitivities appear well suited to discriminating changes in blood oxygen saturation. Trichromatic primates also tend to have bare faces. [verified] — [Changizi et al. 2006, PMC1618887, DOI 10.1098/rsbl.2006.0440](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1618887)
- Related work, not re-fetched: Stephen, Coetzee & Perrett (2011, Evolution and Human Behavior 32:216-227) found that carotenoid (yellow) coloration is preferred over melanin coloration for perceived health. Whitehead, Re, Xiao, Ozakinci & Perrett (2012, PLoS ONE 7:e32988) found that more fruit and vegetables in the diet produced perceptibly healthier skin color within about 6 weeks. [bibliographic, unverified] — [DOI 10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2010.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2010.09.003); [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0032988](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0032988)

### Inferences
- Health and attractiveness judgments respond to blood-color shifts of about ΔE 1.4. Bare redness discrimination needs only about ΔE 0.7. That is below a typical "just noticeable" ΔE of about 1-2.3 for flat patches. It means observers are very sensitive to redness on faces, at least in side-by-side comparisons under controlled conditions.
- The aggression > dominance > attractiveness ordering suggests that how much redness is added carries meaning. Moderate redness reads as health, strong redness as threat. This fits the anger-redness perception findings in section 3.

### Gaps
- Most stimuli in the Perrett group's work were young, mostly white faces. The 2009 cross-cultural test is a partial exception. Thresholds for darker skin were not established in the sources retrieved.
- The ΔE in these studies is often defined on the manipulated axis inside face masks. Comparing it directly with natural changes needs care.

## 3. Facial color and perceived emotion (non-Benitez-Quiroz studies)

### Takeaway
Many experiments show that redder faces are judged angrier, and faster. Bluish faces are judged sadder. Greener or yellower faces push judgments toward disgust. Color helps most when expressions are ambiguous, for example anger vs disgust, fear vs surprise, or happiness vs sadness. When people set face color for each emotion, they make approach emotions redder and yellower and avoidance emotions less so. Camera-based skin color alone predicted valence with about 77% accuracy in one small study.

### Cited Findings
- Nakajima, Minami & Nakauchi (2017), Scientific Reports 7:41019. The study used morph continua (fear-anger and sadness-happiness) rendered in bluish, natural and reddish skin color. Reddish faces enhanced anger perception and were categorized as angry faster. Bluish faces enhanced sadness perception. Sad faces were also more often judged bluish. [verified via PMC and commentary] — [Nakajima et al. 2017, PMC5259783](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5259783) (DOI 10.1038/srep41019); [Commentary, Frontiers in Neuroscience 2017, PMC5532443](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5532443/)
- The commentary questions stimulus color intensity and notes that no age or race effects were reported. [verified] — [Commentary, Front Neurosci 2017, DOI 10.3389/fnins.2017.00435](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2017.00435/full)
- Minami, Nakajima & Nakauchi (2018), Frontiers in Psychology 9:1012. Face color affected expression judgments significantly more than background color, even though the face color was less saturated. [verified, search abstract] — [Minami et al. 2018, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01012](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01012/text)
- Shibusawa, Hasegawa, Tamura, Nakauchi & Minami (2025), Journal of Vision (bioRxiv preprint 2025.03.06.641772). Faces were shifted from a* +0 to a* +12. The terminal facial color drove anger perception, whether the color change was shown dynamically or statically ("terminal color dominance"). [verified, abstract/search snippet] — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.06.641772.full.pdf); [UMKC listing](https://research.umkc.edu/segal/publications/dynamic-versus-static-facial-color-changes-evidence-terminal-color-dominance)
- Thorstenson, Elliot, Pazda, Perrett & Xiao (2018), Emotion 18(7):1032-1042. Across 4 experiments, participants adjusted face or shape color along the red-green and yellow-blue axes for 6 emotions. They made faces redder and yellower for approach emotions (anger, happiness, surprise) and less so for avoidance emotions (fear, sadness, disgust). The authors say the pattern fits physiological models of emotion. Adjustments were allowed within about ±20 units on a* and b*. [verified, abstract and secondary] — [St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/11741) (DOI 10.1037/emo0000358); [Liao et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695221080778)
- Thorstenson, Pazda, Young & Elliot (2019), Emotion, "Face color facilitates the disambiguation of confusing emotion expressions". Thorstenson, McPhetres, Pazda & Young (2021/2022), Emotion, "The role of facial coloration in emotion disambiguation". Redder faces were more often categorized as angry and greener faces as disgusted. Color also helped separate surprise from fear and happiness from sadness. [secondary] — [Liao et al. 2022, i-Perception](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695221080778); [Durham repository record](https://dro.dur.ac.uk/28619)
- A review by Thorstenson et al., "The social psychophysics of human face color: review and recommendations" (Social Cognition, 2018), surveys methods and calls for physiologically calibrated color manipulations. [verified, title/PDF listing only] — [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Christopher-Thorstenson/publication/324145494_The_Social_Psychophysics_of_Human_Face_Color_Review_and_Recommendations/links/5ac782c90f7e9bcd519346e8/The-Social-Psychophysics-of-Human-Face-Color-Review-and-Recommendations.pdf)
- Ramírez, Fuentes, Crites, Jimenez & Ordoñez (2014), CVPR Workshops, "Color Analysis of Facial Skin: Detection of Emotional State". The premise is that changes in hemoglobin concentration and oxygenation alter facial hue and saturation. On their own spontaneous-expression dataset, skin color features alone classified valence (positive vs negative) with 77.08% accuracy. These are preliminary results. Page numbers differ between CVF and DBLP. [verified] — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_workshops_2014/W14/html/Ramirez_Color_Analysis_of_2014_CVPR_paper.html)
- Schematic faces: when people "imagined" face color, they chose saturated red for anger, pink or orange for joy, bluish colors for sadness, and white for no emotion (Takahashi & Kawabata 2018). In emoticons, warm colors strengthened Angry and cool colors strengthened Sad, in Japanese participants (Liao et al. 2022). [verified] — [Liao et al. 2022, i-Perception](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695221080778)
- A 2021 IEICE technical report (Ishikawa et al.) studied facial blood flow while observers viewed neutral, angry and embarrassed faces, with and without blushing. [verified, abstract only] — [IEICE abstract](https://www.ieice.org/ken/paper/20210218DCCY/eng/)

### Inferences
- The perception literature mostly uses manipulated still images, with a* shifts of +5 to +12 or free adjustment up to ±20. These are often larger than the ΔE 1-3 shifts in the health studies. Laboratory "anger red" may therefore exaggerate natural blushes.
- Color seems to work mainly as a disambiguating cue that adds to facial muscle movement, not as a standalone emotion signal. The exception is valence, which can be classified somewhat above chance from color alone.

### Gaps
- I did not locate the specific "Kato" study on facial color and emotion named in the assignment. Search did not surface it, so it remains unverified.
- I did not retrieve "Jones et al." on emotion. Jones, Porcheron et al. (2016, Body Image, DOI 10.1016/j.bodyim.2016.02.001) is about cheek redness and periorbital luminance as health cues, not emotion. [bibliographic, unverified]
- Krumhuber and colleagues on face color, emotion accuracy and memory appeared only as a manuscript link and was not reviewed: [UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10137464/1/Krumhuber_FaceColorEmotionAccMem_MOEM_R1_AAM.pdf)

## 4. Hemoglobin vs melanin separation (Tsumura et al.), skin-tone dependence, darker skin

### Takeaway
Skin color in images can be split into hemoglobin and melanin components with independent component analysis in optical-density space (Tsumura et al.). Emotion-related blood changes therefore mainly move the hemoglobin axis. Melanin attenuates visible and camera-detected blood signals. Blushes are harder to see on dark skin, and camera blood-signal methods (rPPG) perform worse for the darkest skin types.

### Cited Findings
- Tsumura, Ojima, Sato, Shiraishi, Shimizu, Nabeshima, Akazaki, Hori & Miyake (2003), ACM Transactions on Graphics 22(3):770-779 (SIGGRAPH 2003). The method extracts hemoglobin and melanin components from a single digital camera image. It first removes shading in the optical-density domain, then applies ICA. It assumes that spatial color variation comes from two pigments with spatially independent quantities, and models skin scattering as linear in optical density. It enabled synthesis of faces with altered hemoglobin or melanin. This builds on Tsumura et al. 1999 (JOSA A). [verified] — [Tsumura et al. 2003 project page](https://www.mi.tj.chiba-u.jp/~tsumura/Tsumura/papers/siggraph2003.htm); [PDF](https://www.mi.tj.chiba-u.jp/~tsumura/Tsumura/papers/TsumuraSig03.pdf); [JOSA 1999 ICA](https://www.mi.tj.chiba-u.jp/~tsumura/Tsumura/papers/JOSA99_ICA.htm) (DOI 10.1145/882262.882344, [bibliographic, unverified])
- Drummond & Lim (2000), Personality and Individual Differences: "The significance of blushing for fair- and dark-skinned people". Blushes are harder to detect on dark skin, so the experience of blushing may differ. How visible a blush is affects the emotions felt in embarrassing situations. [verified, abstract] — [Murdoch record](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-significance-of-blushing-for-fair-/991005541001307891)
- Nowara, McDuff & Veeraraghavan (2020), CVPR Workshops. This meta-analysis covered 73 people and more than 400 videos across 3 datasets. Heart-rate accuracy of camera photoplethysmography dropped significantly for the darkest skin type, though measurement remained possible under some conditions. Performance was also slightly lower for women. [verified] — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/html/w19/Nowara_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Impact_of_Skin_Tone_and_Gender_CVPRW_2020_paper.html)
- Dasa & Davies (2026), BMJ Open, field study in Nigeria. Remote PPG blood-pressure screening performed poorly in this darker-skinned cohort. Systolic sensitivity was 0.00 for Fitzpatrick type VI. [verified, search abstract] — [BMJ Open, DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2026-119311](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/16/6/e119311)
- Contrary evidence: one UCLA-linked rPPG benchmark found no significant performance drop for any skin-tone group. [verified, search snippet] — [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3vr1z05t/qt3vr1z05t.pdf)
- In Stephen et al. 2009, observers of both African and European origin raised redness to make faces of all ethnicities look healthier. [verified] — [Stephen et al. 2009](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0005083)

### Inferences
- Any "emotion from facial color" claim has to account for melanin. A fixed hemoglobin change produces a smaller RGB/Lab change on darker skin, because melanin absorbs strongly in the same visible range. The rPPG skin-tone results are indirect but strong evidence of this. Unbalanced datasets risk biased performance.
- Separating hemoglobin from melanin (Tsumura-style ICA) is the principled route to skin-tone-robust measurement of emotional blood changes. Its validation across Fitzpatrick types was not shown in the sources retrieved.

### Gaps
- I found no study in this session that directly measured emotional blushing ΔE across Fitzpatrick types.
- I did not retrieve validation of Tsumura ICA on darker skin. The original work used mainly Japanese skin images.

## 5. Effect sizes: how large are emotional color changes (ΔE), and are they visible or measurable in ordinary camera footage?

### Takeaway
Perceptual thresholds for blood-related face color are very small: about ΔE 0.7 for redness and about 1.4 for health or attractiveness. Health-optimal adjustments are about ΔE 1.5-3 (a* about +2.4). Pulse-related color changes are far smaller still, yet consumer webcams can measure them with temporal averaging. I found no peer-reviewed measurement of natural emotional blush or anger ΔE in this session. Stimulus manipulations in perception studies (a* +5 to +12) are likely larger than typical spontaneous changes.

### Cited Findings
- The redness discrimination threshold is ΔE 0.67, and the health and attractiveness thresholds are ΔE 1.44 and 1.38. The ColorChecker calibration error of ΔE 1.97 was larger than the redness threshold. This shows that camera calibration error can be as large as the perceptual signals of interest. [verified] — [Re et al. 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0017859)
- Health-maximizing manipulations were: oxygenated-blood axis ΔE +1.52, both axes about ΔE ±3, and cross-cultural a* +2.39. [verified] — [Stephen et al. 2009](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0005083)
- Anger-perception stimuli used a* +0 to +12. [verified, preprint snippet] — [Shibusawa et al. 2025 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.06.641772.full.pdf). Thorstenson et al. 2018 allowed adjustments up to about ±20 on a* and b*. [secondary] — [Liao et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695221080778)
- Real images: Ramírez et al. 2014 extracted skin color from video frames of spontaneous emotion and reached 77% valence accuracy. This shows that emotion-related color is measurable in ordinary video at least to some degree. [verified] — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_workshops_2014/W14/html/Ramirez_Color_Analysis_of_2014_CVPR_paper.html)
- Camera sensitivity to blood: ordinary RGB webcams and consumer cameras detect the cardiac pulse from facial color in ambient light, strongest in the green channel (Verkruysse, Svaasand & Nelson 2008; Poh, McDuff & Picard 2010). Pulse-related color changes are subtler than blushes, which suggests that emotion-scale perfusion changes are within reach of ordinary cameras when exposure and white balance are controlled. [bibliographic, unverified] — [Verkruysse et al. 2008, Opt Express, DOI 10.1364/OE.16.021434](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.16.021434); [Poh et al. 2010, Opt Express, DOI 10.1364/OE.18.010762](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.18.010762). Performance drops for the darkest skin types. [verified] — [Nowara et al. 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/html/w19/Nowara_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Impact_of_Skin_Tone_and_Gender_CVPRW_2020_paper.html)
- Timing: a visible blush appears about 15-20 s after an embarrassing event begins (Shearn et al. 1990). [secondary] — [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00525/full)

### Inferences
- Natural emotional changes, plausibly a few ΔE units in light skin, likely exceed perceptual thresholds. Whether they survive in uncontrolled footage is another matter. Auto white balance, auto exposure, compression, lighting changes and calibration error of about ΔE 2 are all of similar size. Detection in ordinary footage therefore probably needs within-person, within-video baselines and temporal averaging, as rPPG does.
- The perceived effects are larger in perception studies than they would be in the wild, because stimulus a* shifts (+5 to +12) are large compared with health-study shifts.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed source in this session that reports measured CIELab ΔE for real blushing, anger flushing or fear pallor. Shearn 1990 reported coloration changes, but I could not retrieve the values. Other coverage of the topic (for example Benitez-Quiroz 2018, excluded here) should be checked for such numbers.
- I found no direct study comparing human detection of spontaneous blushes in video against camera-measured ΔE.
